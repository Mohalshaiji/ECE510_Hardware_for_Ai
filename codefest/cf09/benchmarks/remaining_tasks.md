# Remaining Tasks Before M4
## ECE 410/510 | Spring 2026

## Task 1

Widen the AXI-S input interface from 64 channels (N_IN=64, 4 flits of 512 bits) to a full spatial tile of B x H x W x C = 64 x 64 x 64 x 64 = 16,777,216 FP32 values per call, or at minimum a row-major batch of H x W = 4,096 spatial locations per call. Currently the interface serializes 262,144 spatial locations one at a time through 4 flits, causing the effective kernel speedup to be 13.7x rather than the 50x analog ceiling. Widening to a 4,096-location-per-call interface reduces the number of AXI-S transactions from 262,144 to 64 per training step, increasing the utilization of the analog crossbar from under 1% to approximately 50% of its capacity. This requires changing N_IN in top.sv from 64 to 64 x 4,096 = 262,144 and updating the flit count in interface_mod.sv from 4 to 16,384 flits, or adopting a streaming DMA path.

## Task 2

Add a second reservoir call pipeline stage so that the write transaction for call N+1 begins while the compute phase of call N is still running. Currently the AXI-S interface_mod.sv holds s_axis_tready low during S_COMPUTE, which stalls the host for 40 cycles (T=20, 2 cycles per step) per call. Adding a 64-word input double-buffer (one register bank for the current e_in under computation, one for the incoming e_in of the next call) would overlap the 40-cycle compute phase with the 4-cycle write of the next input. This reduces cycles per call from 55 to 55 minus min(40, 4) = 51, a 7% reduction in latency, and eliminates the interface stall that currently occupies 73% of each 55-cycle call.

## Task 3

Reduce the clock tree power from 27.93 mW to under 10 mW by halving the MAX_FANOUT_CONSTRAINT from 16 to 8 in config.json and restructuring the enable distribution in compute_core.sv. The post-route power report shows the clock network consuming 27.93 mW, which is 50.5% of the total 55.27 mW digital power. The root cause is that CTS inserted 1,070 buf_12 buffers to drive 4,117 flip-flops from a single clock root, because the high-fanout enable tree forced OpenROAD to use buf_12 (the highest drive-strength cell in sky130_fd_sc_hd) throughout the clock spine. Reducing MAX_FANOUT_CONSTRAINT to 8 in config.json causes Yosys to insert additional buffer levels during synthesis before CTS runs, giving CTS a flatter starting tree with lower per-buffer fanout. This allows CTS to use a mix of buf_4 and buf_2 cells instead of buf_12 uniformly, which reduces switching power because buf_4 has approximately 3x lower output capacitance than buf_12 on sky130A. Additionally, the core_start and channel-select enable signals in compute_core.sv should be registered one cycle earlier so they arrive at their fanout cone with a full clock period of margin, removing the need for CTS to add compensating high-drive buffers on those nets specifically. The expected outcome is a clock tree of approximately 600 buffers at lower average drive strength, reducing clock network power by roughly 60% and bringing total chip power from 55.27 mW to approximately 33 mW without any change to functional behavior or clock frequency.
